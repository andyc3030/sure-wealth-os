# Wealth OS Research, Analysis, Advice and Recommendation Framework

## Objective

The Wealth OS may research, analyse, advise and recommend, but it remains **advisory-only**. It must improve an investment thesis by challenging it, identifying missing risks, finding contrary evidence, and translating evidence into portfolio implications.

The framework is designed for a high-conviction, aggressively positioned but evidence-disciplined global portfolio.

## Seven research themes

1. **Asset-manager concentration**
   - voting power and ownership concentration among BlackRock, Vanguard, State Street and peers;
   - passive versus active ownership;
   - common ownership / horizontal shareholding;
   - proxy voting and stewardship;
   - competition, pricing and corporate-behaviour effects;
   - private-market concentration among Blackstone, Apollo, KKR, Brookfield, Carlyle and peers;
   - antitrust/regulatory issues;
   - moat versus systemic-vulnerability implications.

2. **Debt / monetary regime**
   - sovereign debt sustainability;
   - fiscal dominance;
   - inflation/disinflation regimes;
   - monetary debasement;
   - real yields and term premium;
   - reserve-currency dynamics;
   - Treasury-market structure;
   - central-bank balance sheets;
   - debt-service burden;
   - financial repression;
   - liquidity cycles;
   - dollar dominance and regime shifts;
   - fiscal/monetary interactions with asset prices.

3. **AI adoption / productivity**
   - measured productivity gains;
   - enterprise adoption;
   - labour displacement versus augmentation;
   - capex returns;
   - diffusion rates;
   - software versus infrastructure economics;
   - power demand and data-centre economics;
   - inference versus training economics;
   - profitability/monetisation;
   - sector adoption;
   - general-purpose-technology comparisons;
   - capability claims versus demonstrated economic outcomes.

4. **Bitcoin / financial system**
   - monetary properties;
   - adoption/network effects;
   - institutional ownership and ETF/ETP flows;
   - liquidity/market structure;
   - correlation with risk assets;
   - inflation-hedge and monetary-regime-hedge evidence;
   - sovereign/regulatory adoption;
   - stablecoins, tokenised deposits and CBDCs;
   - mining economics and energy;
   - disintermediation;
   - systemic risks and drawdowns;
   - portfolio-allocation evidence.

5. **AI cooling / power management**
   - rack and accelerator power density;
   - liquid/direct-to-chip/immersion cooling;
   - thermal management;
   - power distribution and UPS;
   - cooling efficiency/PUE;
   - electricity and water constraints;
   - transformer/switchgear bottlenecks;
   - hyperscaler capex;
   - compare Vertiv, Schneider Electric, Eaton, Johnson Controls, Trane, Carrier and relevant suppliers.

6. **Grid / electrification**
   - transmission/distribution capex;
   - transformer shortages;
   - interconnection queues;
   - generation capacity;
   - electrification;
   - utility capex;
   - industrial and data-centre load growth;
   - renewable integration;
   - grid modernisation;
   - nuclear/gas/renewables implications;
   - switchgear/power-management demand;
   - infrastructure backlogs and supply chains;
   - compare Eaton, Schneider Electric, ABB, Siemens, GE Vernova, Hitachi Energy and relevant peers.

7. **AI networking**
   - AI cluster networking;
   - Ethernet versus InfiniBand;
   - switching/optical/interconnect bandwidth;
   - cluster scaling;
   - hyperscaler capex;
   - networking bottlenecks;
   - merchant silicon;
   - network operating systems;
   - switching margins;
   - customer concentration and competitive threats;
   - compare Arista, Cisco, Nvidia networking, Broadcom, Juniper/HPE, Celestica, Coherent, Marvell and relevant optical/interconnect companies.

## Research standard

Sources are ranked primarily by institutional credibility and evidence strength, not popularity or narrative fit.

Preferred hierarchy:

1. peer-reviewed academic work and major university research centres;
2. central banks, governments and multilaterals;
3. primary-data institutions and regulators;
4. major institutional research, with commercial conflicts disclosed;
5. company filings, audited disclosures, earnings calls and investor materials;
6. high-quality specialist/industry research with transparent methodology;
7. reputable financial journalism;
8. expert podcasts/interviews only where claims can be independently verified;
9. promotional/opinion material only as low-tier context.

A podcast, YouTube interview or opinion article must not outrank strong primary or peer-reviewed evidence merely because it is more recent or persuasive.

## Mandatory per-theme source coverage

Before a theme can be marked research-complete:

- at least **10 accepted sources**;
- at least **2 primary or institutional sources**;
- at least **1 academic or research-institution source** where available;
- at least **1 high-quality industry source**;
- at least **3 credible counter-thesis sources**;
- each important claim must be independently cross-checked against at least **2 accepted publishers**.

The system selects the **3–5 strongest sources per theme** after comparison.

## Evidence classification

Every research claim is classified as one of:

- factual evidence;
- forecast;
- opinion;
- assumption;
- promotional material;
- investment recommendation.

These categories must never be silently merged.

If evidence is conflicting, the output must say so.

If evidence is inadequate, use:

**INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE**

rather than filling the gap.

## Counter-thesis requirement

Every theme must include at least three credible sources capable of weakening or falsifying the thesis.

Examples include:

- asset-manager concentration may create regulatory, competition or valuation risk;
- sovereign debt may remain sustainable materially longer than expected;
- AI productivity may disappoint despite capex;
- Bitcoin may remain an unreliable inflation hedge;
- cooling demand may normalise after the capex cycle;
- grid projects may be delayed by regulation, permitting or financing;
- AI networking economics may be competed away by hyperscalers, merchant silicon or alternative architectures.

Contradictory evidence is not suppressed.

## Per-theme investment translation

Each `ResearchThemeAssessment` must answer:

1. what the evidence says;
2. what is genuinely uncertain;
3. what would falsify the thesis;
4. which economic bottlenecks appear structurally attractive;
5. which listed companies appear to offer the highest-quality exposure;
6. which companies may be narrative beneficiaries rather than durable economic beneficiaries;
7. which exposures are already adequately represented in the portfolio;
8. which new exposures, if any, deserve consideration.

## Recommendation requirements

Allowed advisory actions:

- **increase**
- **hold**
- **reduce**
- **exit**
- **consider**
- **avoid**

Every proposed security recommendation must record:

- ticker where applicable;
- company;
- theme role;
- valuation / price context;
- preferred entry zone where evidence supports one;
- downside / invalidation level where evidence supports one;
- 3–5 year structural thesis;
- near-term catalyst;
- principal risks;
- overlap/correlation with existing holdings;
- competitive position;
- capital intensity;
- cash-generation quality;
- balance-sheet quality;
- whether it belongs in the core allocation or an aggressive-scenario sleeve;
- whether it is already held, missing or unknown in the portfolio.

A new holding is **not** recommended merely because it fits a theme.

## Price and valuation discipline

No invented prices.

If current price is stored in a recommendation, the recommendation must also store:

- currency;
- price as-of timestamp;
- immutable raw source provenance.

If current valuation evidence is unavailable or stale, the recommendation must not invent an entry zone or invalidation level.

## Recency

- prioritise 2024 onward for fast-changing evidence;
- retain older foundational work where necessary;
- store publication date where available;
- distinguish structural evidence from rapidly changing market conditions;
- use newest available filings/data for current recommendations.

## Top 25 source set

A final research run creates a reproducible Top-25 set:

- minimum 3 sources per theme;
- maximum 5 sources per theme;
- tier-first ranking;
- within tier, higher research-quality score ranks first;
- source date is a secondary tie-breaker, not a substitute for evidence quality.

## Execution boundary

Research, advice and recommendations are **not orders**.

The Wealth OS must not convert an investment recommendation into:

- a trade;
- an order;
- a transfer;
- a payment;
- a withdrawal;
- borrowing;
- any other financial mutation.

Any future execution system would require a separately designed authorization architecture and explicit human confirmation. It is outside the current Wealth OS scope.
