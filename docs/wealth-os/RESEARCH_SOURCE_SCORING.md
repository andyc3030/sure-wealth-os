# Research Source Scoring and Ranking

## Six 0–5 dimensions

Every source is scored from 0 to 5 for:

1. **Authority**
2. **Evidence quality**
3. **Independence**
4. **Methodological transparency**
5. **Relevance to investment decisions**
6. **Recency**

The stored `overall_quality_score` is the arithmetic mean of the six dimensions, rounded to two decimal places.

This score is **not** the sole ranking key.

## Source tiers

### Tier 1 — highest evidential priority

- peer-reviewed research;
- university research centres;
- central banks;
- government agencies;
- multilateral institutions;
- regulators;
- primary-data institutions.

### Tier 2

- major institutional research;
- company filings;
- earnings calls;
- standards bodies;
- high-quality industry research.

Commercial conflicts must be disclosed.

### Tier 3

- specialist research with transparent methodology;
- reputable financial journalism.

### Tier 4 — context only unless independently verified

- podcasts/interviews;
- opinion;
- promotional material.

## Ranking order

The system ranks by:

1. **source tier ascending**;
2. **overall quality score descending**;
3. **publication date descending**.

Therefore a Tier-4 podcast cannot outrank a Tier-1 primary source merely because the podcast scores well on relevance or recency.

## Downgrade / reject rules

Downgrade or reject sources that are:

- primarily promotional;
- anonymous or poorly sourced;
- dependent on unverifiable claims;
- circularly citing each other;
- materially conflicted without disclosure;
- predominantly social-media speculation;
- sensational without underlying primary evidence.

## Commercial conflicts

Institutional research and company materials can be useful but must record:

- whether a commercial conflict exists;
- conflict notes;
- source type;
- independence score.

A company filing may be authoritative for audited financial facts while remaining non-independent for management forecasts or promotional claims.

## Cross-checking

Important claims require evidence from at least two independent accepted publishers.

Two articles repeating the same original source do **not** count as independent confirmation unless they provide genuinely independent evidence.

## Source selection

Per theme:

- minimum accepted sources: 10;
- primary/institutional: 2;
- academic/research: 1;
- industry: 1;
- counter-thesis: 3.

Final source outputs:

- 3–5 strongest sources per theme;
- 25 strongest sources across all seven themes using the tier-first ranking policy.
